What is a Solid Oxide Fuel Cell Hot Box?   An SOFC Hot Box is an electrochemical conversion device that uses a solid ceramic electrolyte to generate electricity from fuels like hydrogen or natural gas at high temperatures (600–1,000° C).4  The system consists of a stack of cells, each with an anode, cathode, and solid electrolyte, to produce high-efficiency power with low emissions.5    According to Bloom Energy, fuel cells are electrochemical devices which convert chemical energy to electrical energy whereby an electrochemical reaction converts fuel and air into electricity without combustion.  Fuel cells work on hydrogen (H2) or gases containing hydrogen such as natural gas (CH4) for their operation.  They consist of an electrolyte, an anode, a cathode and various interconnects.    With few exceptions, the subject merchandise consists of the necessary parts and components used to construct a complete Solid Oxide Fuel Cell Hot Box.  The diagram submitted by protestant, and included above, indicates that there are additional components necessary to generate electricity and complete the transfer of electricity to an external circuit.  These additional components (which are not the subject of this ruling), include, fuel cell stacks (or fuel rail assembly), a current collector, an electric braze assembly, ceramic channel and ceramic insulation, and a voltage probe assembly.  Although these six components are not the subject of this ruling, to fully understand the nature and primary purpose of the imported anode and cathode assemblies, it is necessary to exam how these two articles function in conjunction with the other components of the Bloom Energy SOFC hot box.   What is a Heat Exchanger?   In Headquarters Ruling Letter (“HQ”) H312651, dated November 23, 2020, CBP noted that different types of heat exchangers work in different ways, use different flow arrangements, equipment, and design features to affect a change in temperature.  Yet, one thing that most heat exchangers have in common is that they function to directly or indirectly expose a warmer medium to a cooler medium, hence the change in temperature (i.e. a heat exchange).  This is usually accomplished by using a set of tubes, fins, shells or chambers, housed within some type of casing.  In HQ H312651, which involved the classification of plate-fin air-cooled heat exchangers, CBP further noted that a “heat exchanger” is “[A] device for making heat pass between a solid object and a liquid or gas, or between two liquids/gases, without allowing the liquids/gases to mix.”  Another definition for a heat exchanger is a device designed to transfer heat from a hot fluid to a cold fluid through a solid wall that prevents the two fluids from  4 Solid Oxide Fuell Cell Hot Box, Energy.Gov  https://www.energy.gov/fecm/solid-oxide-fuel-cells; see also, Solid Oxide Fuel Cell Technology. https://elcogen.com/technology/solid-oxide-fuel-technology/.  5 Id.

The anode exhaust flows radially inward through a passage to an annular flow passage that passes downward through the anode heat exchanger.  After which, the exhaust air is directed to the cathode feed.  Similarly, the cathode side heat exchanger or Unishell Assembly also completes a heat exchange process but differs in that the incoming air is heated up to 850 degrees Celsius on the inlet side by the exhaust air stream and the exhaust air stream is lowered in temperature by incoming air.  More precisely, the combined exhaust flows radially outward through to an annular exhaust flow passage that passes through the cathode Unishell Assembly to transfer heat from the combined exhaust feed to the cathode feed.  However, the heat exchange functionality is subsidiary to the operations of the anode AFS assembly and the cathode Unishell assembly, which is to contribute to and affect the production of electricity.  In particular, as the flow discussion states, newly reformed fuel from the anode heat exchanger is heated and moves to the bottom of the AFS where it flows through the eight fuel-in fuel rails (back and forth).  This heated fuel passing over and through the fuel rails (fuel cell stacks/columns) is what creates electricity.  Likewise, the flow discussion analysis also establishes that the cathode Unishell assembly also contributes to the creation of electricity and not merely to a change in temperature.    Moreover, as previously noted, in addition to the imported merchandise, a fully assembled SOFC Hot Box includes a fuel rail assembly, a current collector, an electric braze assembly, a voltage probe assembly, ceramic channels and ceramic insulation and a fuel rail assembly; all of which will be assembled post-importation.  These additional components (added post-importation), complete the generation and transfer of electricity out of the SOFC Hot Box to 
  11  an external circuit.  In particular, the electric braze assembly – creates a conductive seal between the ceramic electrolyte and the metallic components of the AFS assembly.10  The voltage probe assembly monitors cell-level health, performance and thermal gradients during high-temperature operation.  The Fuel Rail Assembly, which contains the fuel cell stacks, is where the electricity is generated by the fuel/air passing from the AFS.  Finally, the current collector, a highly conductive thermally resistant component, gathers the electricity generated by the fuel cell stack and transfers it to an external circuit.11  Indeed, as the operational diagram and production flow discussion establishes, the creation of electricity is accomplished by the combined functioning of the anode AFS assembly, the cathode Unishell Assembly, and the fuel rail assembly along with the aforementioned ancillary components.  As such, the facts before us establish that the sole and principal function of the Bloom Energy SOFC Hot Box is to produce electricity.  Not only is the temperature change performed by the anode and cathode assemblies subsidiary to the function of creating electricity, but these assemblies also perform additional functions which are beyond the scope of heat exchangers of heading 8419, HTSUS.  For instance, the anode heat exchanger assembly reforms the inflowing fuel.  As previously stated, new heated exhaust fuel flows through the gas reformer (“OGRE”) in order to remove extra carbon atoms from the fuel.  This process of reforming fuel is not a function described by the terms of heading 8419, HTSUS, and is not an operation that comes with the definition of a heat exchanger.  On the cathode side, cathode exhaust mixes with a percentage of the anode exhaust stream and flows through a catalyst that converts CO to CO2.  Converting carbon dioxide to carbon monoxide is also not a function described by the terms of heading 8419, HTSUS, and is also not an operation attributed to heat exchangers.  In sum, although these additional functions contribute to the overall functioning of the SOFC Hot Box, they are not operations that are described by the terms of heading 8419, HTSUS.  Thus, neither the anode nor cathode assembly parts are included in heading 8419, HTSUS, and must be classified according to the terms of Note 2(b) to Section XVI rather than Note 2(a) to Section XVI, HTSUS.    CBP has previously addressed situations where heat exchange was subsidiary to the primary function of a machine for purpose of Note 2(A)(v) to Ch. 84, HTSUS.  For instance, in HQ H293457, dated April 29, 2019, CBP determined that a machine was precluded from classification under heading 8419, HTSUS, because the heating process was subsidiary to the machine’s primary purpose.  In HQ H293457, the primary function of a recycling machine was to recycle trims and scrap of thin polyester film into granules of polyester for use in the manufacture of biaxially oriented polyethylene terephthalate (“BOPET”).  Heat was used to melt polymer flakes into polymer.  However, HQ H293457 found that the role of heating, while necessary to melt the polymer flakes, was subsidiary to the primary purpose of recycling. Instead, the heating exchange process performed by the anode and cathode assemblies contribute to the principal function of creating electricity.  As such, the anode and cathode assemblies are precluded from classification in heading 8419, HTSUS, by application of Note 2(A)(v) to Chapter 84, HTSUS.     Fuel Cell Machines & Their Parts  Fuel Cell Generators such as the Bloom Energy SOFC, are described by the terms of heading 8501, HTSUS, as they are machines that produce electrical power from various energy sources.   For purposes of Note 2(b) to Section XVI, parts suitable for use solely and principally with machines of 85.01 or 85.02 and which cannot be classified under their own headings pursuant to Note 2(a) to Section XVI, are classified in heading 8503, HTSUS.  Inasmuch as the anode and cathode assemblies are not classifiable under their own heading, we must consider their classification as parts of fuel cell machines.     The Bloom Energy SOFC is similar to merchandise that CBP has previously classified as fuel cell electric generators under heading 8501, HTSUS.   For instance, in New York Ruling Letter (“NY”) N313807 dated August 31, 2020, CBP considered the classification of a Fuel Cell Engine and a Fuel Cell Stack.  The Fuel Cell Engine was described as an electrical generator consisting of a Fuel Cell Stack, pumps, valves, compressors, electrical controllers and panels, sensors, which were all mounted together in a tubular steel frame.  The Fuel Cell Engine generated electricity via an electrochemical reaction using hydrogen.  The Fuel Cell Stack was a subassembly of the Fuel Cell Engine and was described as the core of the generator.  It consisted of a stacked assembly of end plates, individual cells, and anode and cathode plates.  The Fuel Cell Stack was said to promote a controlled reaction that converts hydrogen and oxygen from ambient air into DC electricity and water as exhaust.  In NY N313807, CBP found that the Fuel Cell Stack assembly provided the essential character of a fuel cell electrical generator and thus classified the fuel cell engine and the fuel cell stack assembly in heading 8501, HTSUS.  Likewise, in NY J80297, dated February 4, 2003, CBP classified a solid oxide fuel cell power system that used solid-oxide fuel cells to convert fuel into electrical energy under heading 8501, HTSUS.  The SOFC system in NY J80297 contained a fuel cell stack, an inverter gas fuel 
  13  connecting equipment and other components to create a chemical reaction between gas fuel and oxygen to produce kilowatts of power.     Moreover, and similar to the separately imported component parts in the instant case, NY E86434, dated September 20, 1999, addressed the classification of fuel cell electrical generator parts that were imported separately.  In NY E86434, fuel cell stacks were imported separately from other components.  The completed machine, assembled post-importation, was described as a Proton Exchange Membrane Fuel Cell Power Plant.  The fuel cell power plant described in NY E86434 used a fuel processor to extract hydrogen from natural gas.  The hydrogen was fed into a fuel stack, which generated water, heat and DC electricity.  An electrical inverter converted the DC current into 250kW AC current.  Based on their role in the completed fuel cell power plant, the fuel cell stacks were classified as parts of an AC electric generator in heading 8503, HTSUS.   The subject anode and cathode heat exchangers generate heat and other by products to contribute to the overall purpose of a SOFC Hot Box.  Once the subject anode and cathode assemblies are combined with the fuel rail assembly and other components, the completed unit generates electricity.  Inasmuch as, the subject anode and cathode assemblies are integral to the functioning of the SOFC Hot Box (a fuel cell electricity generating machine) and are designed and used solely in that SOFC Hot Box, they are to be classified as parts of that machine in heading 8503, HTSUS, pursuant to Note 2(b) to Section XVI.  Because the subject goods are covered by heading 8503, HTSUS, there is no need to address the possible classification under heading 8543, HTSUS.

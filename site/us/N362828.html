Konu

The tariff classification, country of origin, and marking of colored pencils

Eşyanın Tanımı (Türkçe özet)

2 Temmuz 2026 tarihli mektubunuzda, müşteriniz International Stationery Company Limited adına sınıflandırma, menşe ülke ve işaretleme kararı talep etmiştiniz. Söz konusu ürünler, her biri 7,0 inç x 0,3 inç boyutlarında ve 4 milimetrelik balmumu/kil çekirdekli 24 yuvarlak renkli kalemdir. Kalemler, çekirdeğin rengine uyacak şekilde boyanmış, uç kesilmiş ve keskinleştirilmiş ahşaptan yapılmıştır. Yüksük veya silgi içermezler. Kalemler, yivli plastik bir parçaya sahip teneke bir kapta paketlenecektir. Mektubunuzda verdiğiniz bilgiye göre, Endonezya'dan gelen ham Pulai ahşabı, çıtalar oluşturacak şekilde şekil ve boyutta kesiliyor ve daha sonra kalem çekirdeklerine uyacak şekilde yiv açıldığı Vietnam'a toplu olarak gönderiliyor. Vietnam'da kalem çekirdekleri Çin'den gelen malzemeler kullanılarak üretiliyor. Balmumu eritilir ve kil, pigmentler ve bağlayıcılarla karıştırılır, daha sonra karışım öğütme, soğutma, toz haline getirme, eskitme, kurşun ekstrüzyon ve kurutma işlemlerine tabi tutularak tamamen şekillendirilmiş kalem çekirdekleri elde edilir. Çekirdekler tutkalla yivli çıtalara yerleştirilir, birbirine kenetlenir ve kurumaya bırakılır. Kuruduktan sonra yapıştırılmış çıtalar kesilip tek tek kurşun kalemler halinde şekillendiriliyor, ardından zımparalanıyor, cilalanıyor ve çekirdek rengine uyacak şekilde boyanıyor. Kullanım için keskinleştirilirler ve kalem gövdesinin üzerine bir logo damgalanır. Kalemler daha sonra Çin menşeli teneke bir kapta paketleniyor ve şrink ambalajlanıyor. Gönderiminize göre, kaplar "Vietnam'da Üretilmiştir" olarak işaretlenecek ve kalemlerin nihai alıcı tarafından işaretlenip görüleceği dış kapta mühürleneceğinden, kalemlerin menşe ülke işaretlemesinden muaf tutulup tutulamayacağını soruyorsunuz.

SINIFLANDIRMA Renkli kalemlerin 9609.10.0000 Uyumlaştırılmış Tarife Alt pozisyonu altında sınıflandırılmasını öneriyorsunuz.

Sınıflandırmanın Gerekçesi (Türkçe özet)

Renkli kalemler için geçerli alt pozisyon 9609.10.0000, HTSUS olacaktır ve bu alt pozisyon, “Kurşun kalemler (9608 pozisyonundaki kurşun kalemler hariç), boya kalemleri, kurşun kalem uçları, pastel boyalar, kara kalemler, yazı veya çizim tebeşirleri ve terzi tebeşirleri: Kurşun kalemler ve boya kalemleri (uçları kılıf içinde). The rate of duty will be 14 cents per gross plus 4.3 percent ad valorem. ORIGIN AND MARKING When determining the country of origin, the substantial transformation analysis is applicable. Bkz. örneğin, 6 Kasım 2018 tarihli Genel Merkez Karar Mektubu H301619. Önemli bir dönüşümün gerçekleşip gerçekleşmeyeceğini belirlemek için yapılan test, bir eşyanın yeni bir isimle, karakterle veya eşyanın işlenmeden önce sahip olduğundan farklı bir kullanıma sahip bir süreçten çıkıp çıkmadığıdır. See Texas Instruments Inc. v. United States, 681 F.2d 778 (C.C.P.A. 1982). This determination is based on the totality of the evidence. See National Hand Tool Corp. v. United States, 16 C.I.T. 308 (1992), aff’d, 989 F.2d 1201 (Fed. Cir. 1993). Ek olarak, 1930 tarihli Tarife Yasası'nın değiştirilmiş şekliyle (19 U.S.C. 1304) 304. Maddesi, istisna olmadıkça, Amerika Birleşik Devletleri'ne ithal edilen yabancı menşeli her eşyanın, eşyanın (veya kabının) niteliğinin izin verdiği ölçüde, Amerika Birleşik Devletleri'ndeki nihai alıcıya eşyanın menşei ülkesinin İngilizce adını gösterecek şekilde, okunaklı, silinmeyecek ve kalıcı olarak göze çarpan bir yerde işaretleneceğini öngörmektedir.

CATEGORY: Classification; Origin; Marking  
TARIFF NO.: 9609.10.0000
  David M. Murphy  GDLSK LLP  599 Lexington Avenue  New York, NY 10022  RE:  The tariff classification, country of origin, and marking of colored pencils  Dear Mr. Murphy:  In your letter dated July 2, 2026, you requested a classification, country of origin, and marking ruling on  behalf of your client, International Stationery Company Limited.   The products under consideration are 24 round colored pencils that each measure 7.0” x 0.3” with a  4-millimeter wax/clay core.  The pencils are made of wood that is painted to match the color of the core, end  cut, and sharpened.  They do not include ferrules or erasers.  The pencils will be packaged in a tin container  with a grooved plastic insert.  According to the information that you provided in your letter, raw Pulai wood from Indonesia is cut to shape  and size to form slats and is then shipped in bulk to Vietnam, where they are grooved to fit the pencil cores.  In Vietnam, pencil cores are produced using materials sourced from China.  Wax is melted and mixed with  clay, pigments, and binders, then the mixture undergoes grinding, cooling, pulverizing, aging, lead extrusion,  and drying to produce fully formed pencil cores.  The cores are inserted into the grooved slats with glue,  clamped together, and allowed to dry.  Once dry, the glued slats are cut and shaped into individual pencils,  then sanded, polished, and painted to match the core color.  They are sharpened for use, and a logo is  stamped onto the pencil barrel.  The pencils are then packaged in a tin container of Chinese origin and shrink  wrapped.   Per your submission, the containers will be marked “Made in Vietnam,” and you ask if the individual pencils  can be exempt from country of origin marking as they will be sealed in the outer container which will be  marked and seen by the ultimate purchaser.  

CLASSIFICATION  You suggest classification of the colored pencils under subheading 9609.10.0000, Harmonized Tariff  Schedule of the United States (HTSUS).  We agree.  The applicable subheading for the colored pencils will be 9609.10.0000, HTSUS, which provides for  “Pencils (other than those pencils of heading 9608), crayons, pencil leads, pastels, drawing charcoals, writing  or drawing chalks and tailors’ chalks: Pencils and crayons, with leads encased in a sheath.”  The rate of duty  will be 14 cents per gross plus 4.3 percent ad valorem.  ORIGIN AND MARKING  When determining the country of origin, the substantial transformation analysis is applicable. See, e.g.,  Headquarters Ruling Letter H301619, dated November 6, 2018.  The test for determining whether a  substantial transformation will occur is whether an article emerges from a process with a new name,  character, or use different from that possessed by the article prior to processing.  See Texas Instruments Inc.  v. United States, 681 F.2d 778 (C.C.P.A. 1982).  This determination is based on the totality of the evidence.    See National Hand Tool Corp. v. United States, 16 C.I.T. 308 (1992), aff’d, 989 F.2d 1201 (Fed. Cir. 1993).  Additionally, Section 304 of the Tariff Act of 1930, as amended (19 U.S.C. 1304), provides that unless  excepted, every article of foreign origin imported into the United States shall be marked in a conspicuous  place as legibly, indelibly, and permanently as the nature of the article (or its container) will permit, in such a  manner as to indicate to the ultimate purchaser in the United States, the English name of the country of origin  of the article.  Congressional intent in enacting 19 U.S.C. 1304 was “that the ultimate purchaser should be  able to know by an inspection of the marking on the imported goods the country of which the goods is the  product.  The evident purpose is to mark the goods so that at the time of purchase the ultimate purchaser may,  by knowing where the goods were produced, be able to buy or refuse to buy them, if such marking should  influence his will.”  See United States v. Friedlaender & Co., 27 C.C.P.A. 297, 302 (1940).  Part 134 of the U.S. Customs and Border Protection (“CBP”) Regulations (19 CFR 134) implements the  country of origin marking requirements and exceptions of 19 U.S.C. 1304.  Section 134.1(b), CBP  Regulations (19 CFR 134.1(b)), defines “country of origin” as the country of manufacture, production, or  growth of any article of foreign origin entering the United States.  Further work or material added to an  article in another country must effect a substantial transformation in order to render such other country the  “country of origin” within the meaning of the marking laws and regulations.  Based on the facts you have submitted regarding the production of the colored pencils, such operations have  effected a substantial transformation resulting in a new name, character and use of the non-originating  components into the finished pencils.  Accordingly, the country of origin of the colored pencils is Vietnam.   Regarding marking, an article is excepted from marking under 19 U.S.C. 1304 (a)(3)(D) and section  134.32(d), Customs Regulations (19 CFR 134.32(d)), if the marking of a container of such article will  reasonably indicate the origin of such article.  Accordingly, if Customs is satisfied that the article will remain  in its container until it reaches the ultimate purchaser and if the ultimate purchaser can tell the country of  origin of the pencils by viewing the retail container in which it is packaged, the individual pencils would be  excepted from marking under this provision.  Thus, the marking of the tin containers with “Made in  Vietnam” is acceptable and the individual pencils will not need to be marked.  The holding set forth above applies only to the specific factual situation and merchandise description as  identified in the ruling request. This position is clearly set forth in Title 19, Code of Federal Regulations  (CFR), Section 177.9(b)(1).  This section states that a ruling letter is issued on the assumption that all of the  information furnished in the ruling letter, whether directly, by reference, or by implication, is accurate and  complete in every material respect.  In the event that the facts are modified in any way, or if the goods do not 

conform to these facts at time of importation, you should bring this to the attention of U.S. Customs and  Border Protection (CBP) and submit a request for a new ruling in accordance with 19 CFR 177.2.   Additionally, we note that the material facts described in the foregoing ruling may be subject to periodic  verification by CBP.  This ruling is being issued under the provisions of Part 177 of the Customs and Border Protection  Regulations (19 C.F.R. 177).  A copy of the ruling or the control number indicated above should be provided with the entry documents  filed at the time this merchandise is imported. If you have any questions regarding the ruling, please contact  National Import Specialist Sandra Walia at sandra.s.walia@cbp.dhs.gov.
